A usability study with individuals with Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) revealed critical navigation and search-relevance failures on Washington 211's housing resource site — where users in crisis were choosing to abandon the site and call in instead. WA211 acknowledged the findings, with one team member confirming the search-discoverability fix as a known issue.

02.1Research Question & Direction

Research Question

How does WA211's information architecture and UI elements enhance or deter usability for users with TBI?

Question Direction

Does navigation & hierarchy help users locate services without distraction?

Map the site's IA & key nav points against real user needs.

Does the site support completing tasks to find crisis resources?

Surface pain points in the resource-search flow.

Which elements cause the most cognitive friction or fatigue?

Turn findings into actionable recommendations.

03.1Finding — Navigation Confusion

Major · Severity 3

Users could not find where to start their search. The primary search function required a non-standard 3-step path, buried in a visually busy, low-contrast homepage.

04 Reflection

Lessons learned from running sessions with participants who have Traumatic Brain Injury.

Scheduling
Issues

Pts would often “forget” about session

Solution
  1. Source better scheduling software
  2. Include more reminders/notifications
  3. Have a reminder email + joining instructions earlier
Troubleshooting
Issues

Some pts would encounter challenging troubleshooting issues

Solution
  1. Develop better troubleshooting instructions
  2. Prepare for multiple devices
Screening Criteria
Issues

Pts used multiple devices we weren’t prepared to collect data or troubleshoot with.

Solution
  1. Incorporate device question
  2. Refine tech literacy questions
